Incident guide

Multiple Miners Offline

When many miners change state together, the timing and shared infrastructure often matter more than any one miner's error. Treat the event as an incident first and a pile of individual hardware failures second.

Start with blast radius

Identify exactly which miners changed state, when they changed, and what they share: rack, switch, subnet, PDU, breaker, container, pool endpoint, firmware cohort, or cooling zone. A common boundary can explain many symptoms at once.

Do not collapse different states

  • Unreachable is not the same as reachable with zero hashrate.
  • Reachable does not prove pool authorization or accepted shares.
  • A scanner timeout does not prove a miner is powered off.
  • Missing telemetry during an incident should remain Unknown.

Evidence that helps separate causes

Compare miner timestamps with switch/network evidence, power or PDU readings, rack/container location, pool state, recent configuration or firmware changes, and unaffected miners in the same facility. An unaffected comparison group is often as informative as the failed group.

Example

If 47 miners behind one network segment disappear within the same minute while miners on adjacent segments remain normal, that pattern supports a shared network-path hypothesis more strongly than 47 independent control-board failures. The next safe check is to verify the common network boundary and compare another segment before touching miner configuration.

Investigate the incident from your evidence

Attach the scanner export, logs, screenshots, network results, or other evidence you intentionally want analyzed. The Troubleshooter can compare the supplied records without taking control of the miners.
