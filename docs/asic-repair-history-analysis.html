ASIC Repair History Analysis

A repair ticket says what was done. A repair history can show whether the same symptom returned, whether failures cluster by component or cohort, and whether the available evidence supports calling a repair successful.

Preserve asset identity

Repair analysis only works when events can be tied to a stable miner or component identity. Keep serial number, internal asset ID, model/variant, timestamps, symptom description, repair action, and post-repair observation distinct. Do not merge records merely because two miners have similar names or IP addresses.

Look for recurrence, not just counts

  • Did the same symptom return after the repair?
  • How long passed between repair and recurrence?
  • Did a different symptom appear on the same miner?
  • Do repeated failures cluster around a model, batch, rack, environmental condition, or repair type?
  • Is there enough post-repair evidence to know the problem stayed resolved?

Separate repair action from repair outcome

A board replacement, PSU swap, cleaning, firmware change, or returned-to-service note proves an action happened. It does not by itself prove that action fixed the original cause. Follow-up telemetry or a sufficiently long no-recurrence period may strengthen the conclusion.

Example

If one miner appears in repair records four times with the same thermal symptom after three different component swaps, the useful finding is recurrence—not simply four repairs. The next safe question is what environmental, power, cooling, or diagnostic evidence was common across those events and what evidence exists after each repair.

Analyze supplied repair history

Attach a sanitized repair export or maintenance history. Bitcoin Mining Troubleshooter can reason over the records you provide while keeping missing events and uncertain outcomes explicit.
