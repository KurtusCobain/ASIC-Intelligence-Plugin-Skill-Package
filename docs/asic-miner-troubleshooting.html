ASIC Miner Troubleshooting

Good troubleshooting starts by separating what the miner actually reported from what you think caused it. Collect enough evidence to distinguish miner hardware, pool, network, power, thermal, and shared-infrastructure problems before changing anything.

Evidence worth collecting

  • Current and historical miner/kernel logs.
  • Hashrate, temperature, fan, board/chip, uptime, share, reject/stale, and pool state.
  • Scanner or management exports with timestamps and explicit units.
  • Network evidence such as DNS, TCP, route, and reachability results you intentionally collected.
  • Power, rack, PDU, breaker, cooling, and repair-history evidence when the problem affects more than one miner.

Common diagnostic mistakes

Failed ping does not prove a miner is dead. Low total hashrate does not prove a hashboard is dead. Scanner success only proves a usable response was received; it does not prove healthy hashing, pool authorization, or accepted shares. Missing data should remain Unknown instead of being silently converted to zero.

Start with scope

Ask whether the problem is one miner, a cohort, a rack, a network segment, a container, or an entire site symptom. Many miners changing state at the same time should trigger shared-infrastructure checks before dozens of independent hardware diagnoses.

Example investigation

Suppose fourteen miners disappear in the same interval. If those miners share a switch, PDU, rack, or upstream network boundary, that common boundary is stronger evidence than fourteen simultaneous independent control-board failures. The next useful step is an observational check of the shared path, not immediate part replacement.

Analyze your own evidence
